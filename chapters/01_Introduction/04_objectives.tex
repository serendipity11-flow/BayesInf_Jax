\section{Objectives and Contributions}
\label{sec:introduction:objectives}
Starting from BayesFlow, we explore the potential of normalizing flows for parameter identification, system identification, and state estimation. Moreover, we aim to bridge the gap between probabilistic inference and control tasks. 
The primary objectives of this dissertation are as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item To investigate the capabilities of normalizing flows for system parameter identification.
    \item To extend BayesFlow for paramter identification to perform system identification and state estimation tasks.
    \item To assess the performance of the proposed models on benchmark datasets and real-world scenarios.
    \item To analyze the interpretability and uncertainty quantification capabilities of the learned models.
    \item To examine the applicability of the identified systems in control tasks.
\end{itemize}
The key contributions of this dissertation can be summarized as:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Enhancement of the original BayesFlow framework to improve robustness, enabling its deployment in real-world applications.
    \item Integration of BayesFlow with sensitivity analysis to facilitate the estimation of parameters with different degrees of identifiability.
    \item Development of Stochastic BayesFlow by combining stochastic normalizing flows with BayesFlow, aimed at robust parameter identification under uncertainty.
    \item Extension of BayesFlow to learn sequence-to-sequence models for system identification and state estimation. 
    \item Proposal of FlowSDE, a new method for learning unknown stochastic dynamical systems by embedding normalizing flows within stochastic differential equations.
    \item Investigation of the compatibility of normalizing flow-based models within control frameworks.
\end{itemize}
