\section{Objectives and Contributions}
\label{sec:introduction:objectives}
The center aim of this dissertation is to leverage the expressiveness, computational efficiency, and mathematical rigor of normalizing flows to develop a unified, data-driven framework for probabilistic system identification, state estimation, and optimal control. The primary objectives of this dissertation are as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item To investigate the efficacy and limitation of normalizing flows in identifying parameters under system observations.
    \item To adapt and extend normalizing flow architectures for sequential data processing, enabling the estimation of hidden, time-varying states in stochastic dynamical systems.
    \item To formulate and evaluate normalizing flows as generative control policies, exploring their potential to provide uncertainty-aware decision-making within optimal control and reinforcement learning frameworks.
\end{itemize}

Building upon these objectives, the key contributions of this dissertation are:
\begin{itemize}
\item Real-World Performance Assessment: A comprehensive examination of a normalizing flow-based Bayesian inference framework in real-world applications, evaluating its robustness, generalizability, and computational advantages over classical estimation techniques.
\item Sensitivity-Guided Inference: The integration of sensitivity analysis with normalizing flows to enable reliable parameter identification in systems where parameters exhibit varying levels of identifiability.
\item Stochastic BayesFlow: The development of a hybrid framework that combines stochastic components with normalizing flows to achieve robust parameter identification under observational and system uncertainty.
\item FlowSSM: The proposal of a novel state-space modeling method that leverages normalizing flows to capture complex, non-Gaussian uncertainty trajectories in stochastic dynamical systems.
\item IMFlow for Robust Control: The extension of normalizing flows to represent stochastic control policies, introducing a framework for integrating generative models directly into the optimal control and reinforcement learning loop.
\end{itemize}
